The argument

Six parts. It moves from a demonstration, to a problem, to a definition, to a mechanism, to a turn — the shape of an essay rather than a trailer.

I
0:00

The untranslatable

buyurun

One Turkish word. Five English glosses arrive and each is struck through. It means all of them; which one depends on the hand that comes with it. The viewer watches translation fail before a single claim is made.

II
0:15

The problem

Roughly seven thousand living languages, instant and free, in every pocket on earth — and no improvement at all in understanding one another. The figure of sorry follows: one word, five meanings, no dictionary that contains the difference.

III
0:42

What a stereotype is

Meaning is not kept in words. It is kept in who is speaking, to whom, what is owed between them, and what everybody present already knows. Discard all of that and something is still left — and the film names it.

IV
1:11

The instrument

The film inverts to black here. Facts do not remove a stereotype, so do not explain — build a place where a person can fail. Three cities, three ways of being foreign, three language models. Nobody is told the barrier is real; they are made to want bread and fail to ask for it.

V
1:41

The turn

The man I understand perfectly has told me nothing true.
The woman I cannot understand at all has never once lied to me.

Understanding is not translation. It is being let in.

VI
1:55

Close

The ground returns to paper — understanding is the light half of the film — and the thesis is stated plainly for the first and only time.

A stereotype is a compression artifact.

Why light and dark

The inversion is structural, not stylistic. It happens exactly twice, and it carries the film's central move: an argument can be read, but a barrier has to be entered.

The design system

Display & body

Literata

Google's reading typeface, drawn for long-form screen text. Editorial rather than decorative, with the Turkish diacritics the subject requires — İstanbul is set correctly, not approximated.

Annotation

IBM Plex Mono

Every label, figure caption and section marker. It gives the film the register of a research document, which is what earns the argument its hearing.

Palette

Paper, ink, and one signal — İznik cobalt, carried over from the game's own palette. Vermilion appears only where meaning fails, never as decoration.

Motion

Text is masked and wiped up from its baseline rather than faded in, on a single expo-out curve. Nothing is linear, nothing bounces, and every page drifts about 1.5% across its life so no frame is truly still.

The six figures

Each is drawn from scratch and animated — none is a screenshot, and none is stock. They are what make the film an argument rather than a mood.

01
buyurun

Five glosses struck out one at a time in vermilion.

02
The five faces of sorry

03
Where meaning is kept

The word on top; five strata of context beneath it, each larger than the word.

04
The compression

340 distinct marks describing a place, crushed into three crude blocks.

05
Three ways of being foreign

Berlin, London, İstanbul as a specification table: the language model and the emotional register of each.

06
A page you cannot read

Ten lines of word-shapes — what text looks like from outside a language — with one word, brot, surfacing in cobalt.

Still open

The film is silent by design so it can be scored to a locked picture. Every cut currently lands on a whole or half second; once music exists, those cuts should move onto its beats, and the two intended silences — the inversion at 1:11 and the line at 1:41 — should be the only places where the score is absent.
