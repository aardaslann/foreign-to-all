# Project overview

Foreign to All explores how people make meaning when language is unfamiliar. Instead of starting with a vocabulary list, the player starts with a social situation: someone needs help, offers something, or expects a response. The player observes, attempts an interpretation, and revises it after seeing what happens.

## From idea to interaction

The protagonist is an alien arriving on Earth. This makes unfamiliarity part of the player's situation: an ordinary counter, train journey, or conversation can become a puzzle about interpretation. The goal gradually shifts from finding a way around to finding a place among people.

The learning loop is **notice → attempt → observe → revise → use elsewhere**. Speech initially appears as glyphs, with familiarity and confirmed meanings treated separately. The discovery panel preserves the speaker, sentence, visible clue, and player action. The notebook keeps that evidence alongside the player's own written interpretation.

London provides a deliberate contrast: English can already be read, but implication and social register still need to be understood. This supports the project's central premise that decoding words and understanding someone are different tasks.

## Player agency and pacing

Berlin establishes the opening. Istanbul, London, Paris, Moscow, Mumbai, Tokyo, and Mexico City can then be visited in any order. Each has a local language, relationships, discoveries, and a celebration. Players may leave an unfinished chapter and return later. After all eight celebrations, the journey returns to Berlin and a chosen friend gives the alien a name.

Optional work, shopping, and exploration provide texture without requiring the player to exhaust every activity. Essential travel has safety nets. Time advances during exploration and travel, but stops for reading and menus; invitations do not expire. These decisions keep attention on interpretation rather than speed or punishment.
