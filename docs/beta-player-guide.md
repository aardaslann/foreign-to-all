# Foreign to All - Desktop Beta

## Start on Windows

Extract the complete ZIP to a folder you can write to, then open
`ForeignToAll.exe`. Godot is not required. This is an unsigned beta;
only run a copy received from someone you trust. Do not disable Windows security
software to run it.

Target: Windows 10/11, 64-bit, keyboard/mouse or a compatible controller,
and an OpenGL 3.3-capable graphics driver. This is a target configuration, not
a measured minimum-performance claim. Linux and macOS packages have their own
`START-HERE.md` with launch requirements and verification limits. Use the archive
for your operating system; the Windows executable does not run natively on them.

## Your Journey

New Journey opens the alien creator before the opening scene. Continue shows
five manual slots and a separate journey checkpoint. Starting again does not
delete manual saves, but later checkpoints belong to the new journey.

Eight cities contain the core beta stories: Berlin, Istanbul, London, Paris,
Moscow, Mumbai, Tokyo and Mexico City. Other destinations are not accessible
in this beta. Money and travel have safety nets; optional work is not a prerequisite
for progressing through the core stories.

Berlin opens and closes the journey; the other seven can be finished in any
order. After each city your current intention lists every city still waiting
and points to the airport you are nearest. When all eight are done it leads
you back to Berlin, to the field where you landed.

You can leave a city's story unfinished and return later. Its progress is kept;
returning to a finished city still offers a free choice of where to go next.

Flights have a short, pausable and skippable plane sequence. Reduced motion uses
a still view. You land inside the destination's airport, with a checkpoint there.
Follow City transfer to reach town. Berlin, London and Istanbul include one onward
rail ride: go straight to the platform and choose a stop without buying a ticket.
The other five terminals connect directly to their city-transfer street entrances.
Returning for a departure does not repeat your first street impression.

Meeting quests name the in-game metro line, stop and district, followed by a
local landmark. After reaching that neighbourhood, the directions focus on the
landmark instead of telling you to take the train again.

The top-left clock shows local time in 24-hour format. A full day takes about
40 minutes of active exploration. Conversations, reading, menus and cutscenes
pause time, as does leaving the game window unfocused. Time does not pass while
the game is closed. Dawn, daytime, sunset and night change the world's lighting;
indoor services remain comfortably lit.

Beds offer morning (08:00) or evening (18:00) rest. Flights advance the clock
by an approximate journey duration and switch to the destination's local time;
the route and flight screen show the arrival time. Trains advance 15 minutes,
or 30 minutes for an included airport transfer. These are game times, not real
flight schedules or seasonal time-zone calculations. Flights leave on request.

There is no fatigue meter, forced bedtime, closing-time lockout or missed
appointment. When a friend asks you to return tomorrow, reaching the following
morning naturally works too. Once ready, that conversation remains available.

Every core city has an optional encounter with someone new where an earlier
word helps with a different task. These become available after the relevant
discovery, without shopping, collecting items or a penalty for declining.
The notebook distinguishes a familiar sound, a meaning found, and a word used
elsewhere. Deliberately taught words and pickup names have pronunciation playback
in all eight core languages. Repeated pickups no longer stop the game.

Common connecting words still begin as glyphs and become readable through
familiarity. Once readable, every word keeps its own light-purple hover box,
including words you already know. Connecting words offer tentative meanings;
these hints are not the same as a confirmed discovery. Amber letters identify
the current discovery target and gold letters identify confirmed meanings.
English remains readable from the start. When a new
meaning emerges from a conversation, an untimed word panel keeps the speaker,
visible clue and your action together. You can write your own guess or choose
Show the meaning immediately. There is no spelling test or wrong-answer penalty.
The answer stays until you continue; your written reading stays in the notebook
alongside the confirmed meaning. Already-discovered words do not repeat the panel.

Each city's celebration has its own illustrated pixel-art setting, with your
customised alien and the local cast. These are cinematic event scenes, not extra
walkable neighbourhoods.

Cities have different conversation and outing rhythms. Follow your current
intention in the notebook; you do not have to clear every activity. At the end,
your chosen friend gives your customized alien a name in the field where you
landed. You can continue exploring, view credits or leave beta feedback. Return
to the pod to revisit that moment with the same name.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Controller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Walk | WASD / arrows | Left stick / D-pad |
| Interact | E / Space | A / bottom face button |
| Back / pause | Escape | B / right face button, Start where bound |
| Notebook | J | Y / top face button |
| Belongings | I | X / left face button |
| Current tasks | Q | Left shoulder |
| District map | Tab / M | Right shoulder |
| Toggle quest arrow | G | Press right stick |
| Hear again | R | Back / Select |

Keyboard actions can be changed under Settings > Controls. Controller labels
follow the standard Xbox layout; labels on other devices may differ. Feedback
text entry requires a keyboard. Menus support pointer and directional focus.

## Interface And Comfort

English is the complete supported beta interface. The other 19 selectable
interface languages are translation previews and can fall back to English.
The languages encountered in the world deliberately remain local languages.

Settings includes text sizes, reduced motion, context hints, glyph preferences,
optional timed replies, separate speech/music/effects volumes and display options.

## Saves And Updates

Saves: `%APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Foreign to All\saves`

Settings and the language preference are in the parent `Foreign to All` folder.
Before replacing a beta build, close the game and back up that folder.
Existing 0.2.0-beta saves are supported. Saves from a newer schema are refused
without replacing your active journey. Keep backups when moving between builds;
loading a newer save in an older build is not supported.

An unavailable save stays visible in Continue. The game will not silently delete
or repair it. Only delete it after deciding you no longer need the file.

## Feedback And Privacy

Pause > Beta feedback saves a local JSON file containing your note, build ID,
city, scene and position. Optional playtime notes add approximate active exploring
and reading times. Nothing is uploaded automatically.

Choose Show saved file and send the file to the person who supplied your beta,
using the same communication channel. Include a screenshot when useful. Avoid
putting personal information in notes. The game needs no account or online service
to play; recorded voices and music are bundled.

## Known Limits Of This Candidate

- The package includes the completed 30-track music set, unchanged.
- Speech is synthetic. Native-language review, measured playthrough pacing and wider
  human playtesting remain unfinished. Two child character voices remain unavailable.
- Translation previews are incomplete. Feedback composition needs a keyboard.
- This guide describes the Windows candidate; the release also provides a Linux archive with its own launch guide. These are beta packages, not signed installers or a final commercial release.
- Pronunciation coverage is checked automatically, but synthetic pronunciation
  and cultural nuance still need native listeners; coverage is not certification.

Credits are available in Settings > Credits and in `CREDITS.txt`.
Engine and font notices are included in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt`.
The exact build and executable checksum are in `build-manifest.json`.
